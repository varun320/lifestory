# `/approach`

**Page intent:** Show intellectual specificity without publishing performance. Convert "he manages money" into "he thinks about it *this way*, not the default way." The reader should leave able to describe his approach in one sentence.

- **Title:** `Approach — The Zermagen Process | Life Story Wealth Planning`
- **Meta description:** `How I manage portfolios: the Zermagen process — active, risk-first management built around the idea that markets run on liquidity, not on a fixed mix of stocks and bonds.`
- **H1:** `How I manage portfolios`

### Sub-line

`The Zermagen process — active, risk-first, reconstituted quarterly.`

### Section — What most people actually want

**Intent:** *Name the reader's own quiet tension out loud before offering a resolution to it. They should feel understood before they feel sold to.*

Most people who come to me want two things that the industry usually treats as opposites.

They want their money to be safer. They have already built what they have and they do not want to lose it.

They also want their money to grow. Inflation does not stop because you retired. Neither does life.

The standard trade-off is that you can pursue one or the other. Take less risk and accept lower returns, or reach for higher returns and accept the drawdowns that come with them. That trade-off is real at the extremes. It is less real in the middle, and the middle is where most of the work happens.

### Section — What I actually do

**Intent:** *One-paragraph operating principle. The reader should be able to quote this back.*

I hold parts of markets that are working — where money is flowing in, not out — and I move away from parts that are not. I hold cash when nothing deserves capital. I use protective positions when conditions call for them. The portfolio is reviewed every quarter and reconstituted from scratch, not tweaked at the margin.

### Section — What I actually watch

**Intent:** *Expose the belief. This is the intellectual differentiator — most advisors do not have an articulated belief about markets. Give the reader something concrete (2022) to remember.*

My belief is that markets run on liquidity — on where money is moving, and why — more than on any fixed mix of stocks and bonds. That belief matters because the standard "diversified" portfolio can fail exactly when you think it will not. In 2022, the stock market fell and the bond market fell alongside it. A traditional 60/40 offered no shelter.

The Zermagen process is built around that reality. Instead of holding a fixed mix and hoping the mix protects you, I follow where money is actually moving between markets, and I move the portfolio with it. The framework has three layers.

### Section — Layer 1 · Narrow the Universe

**Intent:** *Establish that the process starts from the whole board, then narrows. Competence signal: he has considered what to exclude, not just what to include.*

I start with 21 asset classes covering global stocks, bonds, commodities and currencies. Based on current market conditions, I narrow that set to 7. This layer asks the largest question first: *given what markets are actually doing right now, where is it reasonable to be at all?*

`[NEEDS CONFIRMATION: approval to use a simple diagram illustrating 21 → 7 narrowing. Non-performance, purely conceptual. See open-gaps.md.]`

### Section — Layer 2 · Analyze the Constituents

**Intent:** *Show discrimination inside the shortlist. Competence signal: this is not a passive allocation dressed up.*

Within each of the 7 asset classes I look at the components — what actually makes up each class, and how each piece is behaving. The goal at this layer is to filter out excess downside risk and to remove laggards. Being in the right asset class is not enough on its own.

### Section — Layer 3 · Craft the Strategy

**Intent:** *Show that even after selection, there is a further decision about how to hold. Competence signal: the tools available go beyond buy-and-sell.*

The third layer decides how to hold what remains. Depending on the situation I may buy outright, hold cash, sell covered calls, short-sell puts, hold limited futures exposure, or build long/short spreads. The right position is not always a simple purchase.

### Section — What runs through all three layers

**Intent:** *Convert process into values. The reader should recognise these as principles, not marketing bullets.*

- **Risk first.** How much can be lost in a bad year matters more than how much might be gained in a good one. Every decision is made in that order.
- **Built to move.** Positions are chosen to be reconsidered, not to be forgotten. Every quarter the whole portfolio is reviewed against current conditions.
- **Opportunity across markets.** I am not restricted to one asset class or geography.
- **Discretionary authority.** Under our agreement I have the authority to act when action is warranted, without waiting for approval on each move.
- **Cash is a position.** Holding cash is a decision, not the absence of one. When nothing deserves capital, the answer is to hold cash.
- **No leverage.** My clients want their money treated with care. I do not borrow against the portfolio to reach for larger returns.

### Pull-quote

**Intent:** *Second attitudinal statement — belief about markets themselves, not about how he sells. Reinforces the "he thinks about this" positioning.*

> "Money moves between markets. So should your portfolio."

### Section — What this page does not show

**Intent:** *Turn a compliance absence into an intentional posture. The reader should read this and think "of course."*

I do not publish past performance figures, growth illustrations, or backtests on this site. There are specific rules about how advisers may present results, and I would rather have a conversation about them with you directly than post numbers on a marketing page.

# `/fees`

**Page intent:** Neutralise the fee-anxiety of a referred prospect who has been told fees are unusual here. Convert "unusual" into "aligned." Do it without any worked example.

- **Title:** `Fees — Results, not fees | Life Story Wealth Planning`
- **Meta description:** `No management fee. I am paid only on gains above a high water mark and a quarterly hurdle rate. If the strategy does not work, I do not get paid.`
- **H1:** `Results, not fees`

### Section — What does this cost?

**Intent:** *The wait-what hook. Answer the question the reader came here to ask, then explain.*

Potentially nothing.

Most advisers charge a management fee. Whether your portfolio goes up, down, or sideways, the fee is charged. I do not. If the strategy I run for you does not work in a given quarter — if it does not exceed a defined threshold — I am not paid for that quarter. When it does exceed the threshold, I am paid a share of the excess. That is the whole model.

### Section — In my own words

**Intent:** *Legal fidelity. The exact wording is the wording that governs the arrangement.*

The wording below is exactly as I publish it. It is the wording that governs the fee arrangement.

> "We charge no managements fees. We only make money by increasing your wealth. Performance success fees of 30% are assessed quarterly only on excess returns above a high water mark and a quarterly 1.5% hurdle rate."

`[NEEDS CONFIRMATION: fee wording is under counsel review, including the "managements" typo. Reproduce verbatim until counsel returns a corrected version. See open-gaps.md #4.]`

### Section — What that means, in plain language

**Intent:** *Translation without oversimplification. The careful reader should feel respected — not talked down to.*

Two things have to be true before I am paid anything.

- **Your account has to reach a new high.** This is the *high water mark*. If your portfolio drops and later recovers, no fee is charged on the recovered ground — the water mark is the previous peak. I am not paid twice on the same gains.
- **The new high has to be more than 1.5% above the previous one, for that quarter.** This is the *hurdle rate*. Small gains do not trigger a fee.

When both conditions are met, the fee is 30% of the gains above the hurdle. Otherwise, there is no fee for that quarter.

### Section — Why the model is built this way

**Intent:** *Convert an unusual fee structure into a values statement. Alignment is the point, not novelty.*

I built it this way so my incentives line up with yours. If you do well, I do well. If you do not do well, I do not do well. That is how I want the arrangement to work, and it is a large part of why the firm exists.

### Section — A note on incentives

**Intent:** *Pre-empt the sophisticated reader's next objection — "doesn't a performance fee encourage risk-taking?" — before they raise it.*

A fee structure paid on performance can, in theory, encourage an adviser to take more risk than they otherwise would, in the hope of triggering a fee. I am aware of that risk, and it is part of why my approach starts with risk management before anything else. The high water mark also helps: once a portfolio has fallen, I do not get paid on the recovery back to the previous peak, which removes any incentive to gamble the account back.
