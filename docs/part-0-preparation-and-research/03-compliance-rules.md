# 03 · Compliance Rules — read before writing a word

US financial-advisor advertising is regulated. Getting this wrong is a **regulatory problem for a licensed professional**, not an awkward conversation. If you take nothing else from these docs, take this.

## Never put on the site

1. **Client testimonials, reviews, ratings, star widgets** — even as placeholders.
2. **Any performance figure, return, or track record** — no charts, no "clients averaged X", no hypothetical growth illustrations.
3. **Any investment minimum or fee figure** beyond the exact published wording (see `part-1/02-preserved-substance.md`).
4. **Superlatives** — best, top, #1, leading, award-winning, trusted, premier, elite.
5. **Promises or guarantees** about outcomes, safety, or returns. Watch confident phrasing too.
6. **Client names, logos, photos, stories** — even anonymised or composite.
7. **Invented credentials, awards, team members, years in business, AUM, client counts, office locations.**
8. **Stock photos of people** presented as team or clients.
9. **Trust badges, "as seen in" strips, partner logos** you cannot substantiate.

## Do this instead

- Describe **process**, not results.
- State **verifiable facts**: what the firm is, where it is, what happens in what order.
- Use plain description in place of persuasion. **Clarity is the persuasion.**
- Leave a clearly marked `[NEEDS CONFIRMATION: …]` gap rather than invent.
- Send anything uncertain back through Arham → client's counsel decides.
- **Retain every published version.** Industry requires published material be produced on request for years. Commit + tag every published state in git.

## The single most likely mistake

Every instinct from normal web work says a pro-services site needs testimonials, a results section, trust badges. On this site those are the **three most dangerous things you could add**. If you catch yourself reaching for social proof — stop and ask. There are compliant alternatives.

Same instinct says an adviser site needs numbers to feel credible. Resist it. **Credibility here comes from specificity about process, not from figures.**
